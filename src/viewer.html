            How it works

            Frequency to pattern. The drive tone shakes a plate. Each plate has natural modes at fixed frequencies: Bessel zeros for the disk membrane (f ∝ jmn), m² + n² for the square Chladni plate, √(l(l+1)) for the sphere. The engine sums the modes near your frequency, weighted by a damped resonance of sharpness Q, the way a real driven plate responds.

            Pattern to plan. Sand on a Chladni plate settles on the nodal lines, where the plate does not move. Those lines become boulevards. Contour rings follow the amplitude, and gradient streets run from each nodal line up to the crest, so a ring-and-spoke plan grows around every antinode. Amplitude sets density and height; the sign of the wave splits the city into alternating districts.

            Underground. Every building gets a service connection to the nearest street. Sewers run under the streets as a gravity tree toward treatment plants at the edge: each pipe is sized by Manning's equation for its peak flow and laid at the Ten-State minimum slope for its diameter, so runs get deeper downstream until an 8 m limit triggers a lift station. Water mains are looped under every street and fed from elevated tanks, one per 25,000 people, sized for peak hour at 1.5 m/s. A power station feeds substations through 110 kV lines, and 11 kV feeders fan out to every block. Metro tunnels link the district landmarks along the streets, bored below every nearby foundation with 4.5 m of clearance. Lower gravity steepens the sewers and raises the tanks.

            Simulation. Simulate growth founds the city at the strongest antinode near the drive point and spreads it outward along the streets at 3.7 % built-up growth a year; buildings age on Weibull lifetimes and are rebuilt denser where the plate moves most, within the same structural limits. Each keyframe is a complete city with its networks rebuilt. The hour slider runs the networks under load: Hazen-Williams pressure along the water mains, three-phase voltage drop along the feeders, Manning partial-flow depth in the sewers and a gravity-model commute on the metro. Fail this on any asset shows who loses service; the N-1 tiles show the worst single failure per network. The 110 kV grid has two injection points and backup lines routed away from shared trenches, scored per corridor; where a dense arcology cannot reach the 20 % target, the check says so.

            Realism rules. Streets aim for UN-Habitat's 18 km of street per km². Every building keeps a setback from every road and sits within walking reach of a street. Heights stay under the material's self-weight limit σ/(ρ·g·3) and a slenderness limit that eases at low gravity. Population assumes 45 m² per resident and 15 m² per worker.

            What the frequency does not mean. The standing-wave patterns are real physics; turning them into a city is a design choice. No study shows 432 Hz, Solfeggio tones or the Schumann resonance make a place healthier or more harmonious, so the engine treats every frequency alike. Some Neolithic chambers do resonate near 95–120 Hz; whether that was intended is still debated.
