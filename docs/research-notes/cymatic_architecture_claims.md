# Cymatics, Sound and Frequency in Architecture and City Design: Claims and Evidence Map

Source labels used throughout: **[PRIMARY]** original work or direct measurement report; **[ADVOCATE]** promotes the claim; **[CRITICAL]** skeptical or debunking; **[NEUTRAL]** reference, news or fact-check summaries without a stake. Most pages were read via a fetch tool that summarises the page, so wording is paraphrase unless quoted. Where I only had a news or secondary account of a paper, that is stated.

## 1. Hans Jenny, Ernst Chladni, and modern cymatics advocates (CymaScope / John Stuart Reid)

### Takeaway
The visual phenomena are real and fully explained by classical physics (vibration modes, nodal lines, Faraday waves). Jenny's contribution was systematic documentation and naming, plus an interpretive philosophy (anthroposophic, "phenomenological") that goes beyond what the physics shows. Nothing in the sources I found shows Jenny himself made claims about architecture, 432 Hz or city design. CymaScope claims are broad and I found no peer-reviewed support on its own site.

### Cited Findings
- Ernst Chladni systematically presented plate and membrane vibration patterns in 1787 (*Entdeckungen über die Theorie des Klanges*). Earlier observers include Galileo (~1630) and Robert Hooke (documented 8 July 1680). Normal modes and nodal lines are determined by the surface's geometry and how it is constrained. [NEUTRAL] — [Wikipedia: Cymatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cymatics)
- Hans Jenny (1904–1972) was a Swiss physician, a Waldorf teacher and a student of Rudolf Steiner's anthroposophy. He coined "cymatics", and published *Kymatik* vol. 1 in 1967 and vol. 2 posthumously in 1972. He used powders, pastes and liquids on plates driven by an oscillator over a wide frequency range. [NEUTRAL] — [Wikipedia: Cymatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cymatics)
- Jenny interpreted the resulting mandala-like and nature-like forms as manifestations of an invisible "force field of vibrational energy". He was especially struck by a circle with a central point formed in lycopodium powder when "Om" was chanted. The same article says the Om pattern is consistent with known mathematics (circular plates have centrally symmetric nodal modes), that pattern form depends on the vibrating body's shape and the sound's spectrum, and that classical physics best explains the phenomena. [NEUTRAL] — [Wikipedia: Cymatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cymatics)
- Jenny built a "tonoscope" and documented work in photographs and film; his training was medical and he taught science at a Waldorf school in Zurich. [ADVOCATE foreword, admirer] — [Honest Broker](https://www.honest-broker.com/p/the-magical-and-musical-research). My read of that piece: it cites no controls, data or peer-reviewed sources for Jenny's results (evidence is mostly photographs and film), and its links to consciousness, healing and creation myths are the author's interpretation, not demonstrated.
- Frank Perry (sound-healing practitioner) says Jenny worked ~14 years with calibrated frequencies and a crystal oscillator, links cymatic figures to the harmonic series ("a law of nature"), Pythagoras and Fludd, and says Jenny did not, as far as he knows, apply findings to healing. The healing link is the author's extrapolation. The article does not mention architecture and offers no controlled experiments. [ADVOCATE] — [Frank Perry](https://www.frankperry.co.uk/about/articles/articles-written-by-frank-perry/cymatics/)
- The CymaScope site describes the device as "the first scientific instrument that can provide an analog image of sound and vibration", frames cymatics as Faraday-wave science, and lists 16 application fields (including Quantum Physics, Sound Therapy, Cardiography, Asteroseismology). The landing page gives no studies, citations or data. [ADVOCATE, primary vendor page] — [CymaScope](https://www.cymascope.com)
- Reid and Larson began developing the CymaScope in 2002 (latex membrane first, then ultra-pure water). They call it "the first truly scientific cymatics instrument" and point to uses in acoustical analysis of the Great Pyramids, dolphin communication and sound healing. A blog describing it provides no peer-reviewed evidence for those claims and notes only an unexplained pause in the A2 demo pattern; the blogger says they are "certainly no expert". [ADVOCATE claims relayed by a lay blogger] — [CR4/GlobalSpec blog](https://cr4.globalspec.com/blogentry/22054/The-CymaScope-Scientific-Tool-or-Aural-Paintbrush)
- Physics of the water patterns: Faraday (1831) showed standing waves/regular patterns in vibrated liquids ("Faraday waves"). Pattern shape and stability depend on frequency, amplitude, vessel shape, device resonance, environment and water properties. [NEUTRAL conference abstract, ECSIA 2025] — [Kubínová & Bednář](https://ecsia2025.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/abstracts/Kubinova.pdf)

### Inferences
- A design engine can honestly say "inspired by Chladni/Faraday standing-wave geometry" (mathematically well-defined, reproducible). It cannot honestly say Jenny or CymaScope showed that such patterns confer spiritual, health or "harmonic" benefits on buildings or cities.
- Jenny's "force field" interpretation is philosophical, not an experimental finding. Classical physics predicts the same patterns without it.

### Gaps
- I did not access Jenny's original *Kymatik*/*Cymatics* text; Jenny claims here are from secondary or advocate summaries.
- I found no independent peer-reviewed validation (or refutation) of specific CymaScope claims (pyramid analysis, dolphin, healing). The CymaScope site's research section appeared to be only topic links. Reid's published pyramid claims were not located (see Q2).

## 2. Claims linking 432 Hz, Solfeggio frequencies, Schumann resonance (7.83 Hz) to sacred architecture, ancient sites, ideal cities

### Takeaway
These claims have weak or no documented basis. 432 Hz and Solfeggio "frequencies" trace to 20th-century advocacy movements, not ancient practice; Schumann resonance is a real atmospheric phenomenon but the human-health/architecture link is unsupported in peer-reviewed literature. One small human study on 432 vs 440 Hz exists and is weak. Absence of evidence is not proof of falsity for health effects, but the "ancient architecture was tuned to X Hz" framing lacks any documentary basis I could find.

### Cited Findings
**432 Hz**
- No 432 Hz standard existed before 440 Hz; European pitch ranged ~409–457 Hz over time. 440 Hz was proposed at the Stuttgart Congress of Physicists in 1834 (Scheibler); France adopted 435 Hz in 1859; the American Federation of Musicians adopted 440 in 1917; a 1939 London conference recommended 440; ISO specifies A=440. [NEUTRAL fact-check] — [Lead Stories](https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2022/10/fact-check-tuning-standard-of-a-440-hz-was-not-driven-by-nazi-and-rockefeller-desire-for-discord.html)
- Verdi used 435 Hz for the Requiem (1874) and reportedly said he slightly preferred 432 Hz; a 1780 Mozart-linked fork measured ~421.6 Hz. The Goebbels and Rockefeller narratives trace to Leonard Horowitz and Lyndon LaRouche's Schiller Institute; Lead Stories found no evidence for them. [NEUTRAL/CRITICAL] — [Lead Stories](https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2022/10/fact-check-tuning-standard-of-a-440-hz-was-not-driven-by-nazi-and-rockefeller-desire-for-discord.html)
- "Scientific pitch" sets C4=256 Hz (powers of two), which gives A4 ≈ 430.54 Hz, not 432. The Schiller Institute campaigned from 1988 for A=432 as "Verdi tuning". Wikipedia states there is no scientific evidence of 432 Hz healing benefits. [NEUTRAL] — [Wikipedia: Scientific pitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_pitch)
- Only one study directly comparing 432 vs 440 Hz is cited by ScienceAlert (PubMed 31031095, 2019): movie soundtracks at 440 on one day and 432 on another; 432 sessions followed by slightly lower heart rate and blood pressure. Very small sample, not randomised, so expectation/relaxation effects not excluded. The claim that 432 is closer to "natural harmonics" is an advocate claim the article does not evaluate. [NEUTRAL journalism] — [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/432hz-brain-tuning-is-an-ancient-idea-does-it-actually-work)
- Skeptoid calls the claim that 432 Hz matches the Great Pyramids and the Sun "patently absurd" (opinion piece). [CRITICAL] — [Skeptoid ep. 474](https://skeptoid.com/episodes/474)

**Solfeggio frequencies (396, 417, 528, 639/629, 741, 852 Hz)**
- Joseph Puleo, an herbalist, reportedly derived them in the 1970s from a verse-number pattern in the Book of Numbers. The name is borrowed from solfège (11th-century syllables from a Gregorian hymn); the two are otherwise unrelated. No evidence they were sung in ancient chants. The review rates health claims "Unsupported" ("no scientific or clinical evidence for tones at 741 Hz or other so-called Solfeggio frequencies", 2022-09-30). [CRITICAL fact-check] — [Science Feedback](https://science.feedback.org/review/no-evidence-support-claim-solfeggio-frequencies-remove-toxins-from-body/)
- Skeptoid traces the syllables to Guido of Arezzo's *Ut queant laxis* and notes they were not tied to fixed frequencies; proponents disagree among themselves about which frequency matters. [CRITICAL] — [Skeptoid ep. 474](https://skeptoid.com/episodes/474)

**Schumann resonance (~7.83 Hz)**
- Peer-reviewed work uses Schumann resonances as a remote-sensing tool for lightning and the ionosphere; it has not established links to human physiology or time perception. Wellness claims (tuning to 7.83 Hz ≈ alpha band) spread in New Age/wellness communities from the mid-2010s; products include "Schumann resonators". Some 2025 work (Nelson; Nevoit et al.) explores possible subtle effects on bioelectricity or sleep, described as tentative and debated. Critics include Harriet Hall (2011), Ethan Siegel (2023). [NEUTRAL summary with critical citations] — [Wikipedia: Schumann resonances conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schumann_resonances_conspiracy_theories)
- The wavelength at 7.83 Hz in air is roughly 43 km (speed of sound ~343 m/s), so it cannot be a standing-wave or room resonance of any building (my arithmetic, not from a source). Schumann resonance is electromagnetic, not acoustic.

**Sacred geometry / harmonic proportion in architecture (historical, as distinct from Hz claims)**
- Musical-ratio proportion theory in architecture is documented historically: René Ouvrard's *Architecture Harmonique* (1679) maps musical consonances onto architectural ratios; Wittkower is the standard modern reference. Taraldsen (2023) finds the mathematical correlations are internally accurate, but the system's claimed infallibility rests on sensory judgment and is not sustained. This is a documented design *tradition* (a ratio system), not evidence that buildings sound or "resonate" at particular pitches. [NEUTRAL scholarly] — [Taraldsen, Nexus Network Journal](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00004-022-00625-9)
- Rosslyn Chapel: Stuart and Thomas Mitchell claim carved cubes encode Chladni-like patterns that form a "Rosslyn Motet". Critics: Slate's author notes the Motet "sits on top of a mountain of shaky assumptions"; music professor Warwick Edwards said it resembled no medieval music he knew; a stonemason said no more than 7–8 of 213 cubes were displaced; with only 12 unique symbols a cipher seems unlikely; the Chladni-matching is questioned. Author is undecided overall. [CRITICAL/NEUTRAL journalism] — [Slate](https://www.slate.com/articles/life/the_rosslyn_code/2011/05/the_rosslyn_code.html); advocate claim also reported in [Wikipedia: Cymatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cymatics) (hedged).

### Inferences
- Any engine output saying "432 Hz / 528 Hz / 7.83 Hz is the harmonic frequency of this layout" should be labelled as symbolic or aesthetic, not evidential. A city plan has no characteristic frequency in the audible band in any meaningful single-pitch sense, and a Schumann-based design would have no acoustic meaning.
- The ratio-based proportion tradition (Ouvrard/Wittkower) is the best-documented historical link between "harmony" and architecture, and is legitimately citable as a historical design idea.

### Gaps
- I did not find a primary source (Puleo, Horowitz) directly; origin story is from fact-checkers.
- I did not find any peer-reviewed study testing Solfeggio frequencies in architectural settings.
- I did not locate the original Schumann-related physiological studies (e.g., König, Wever) to assess them.
- The 2019 432-vs-440 study was read only via ScienceAlert's description; exact sample size not obtained.

## 3. Archaeoacoustics: measured resonances (Newgrange ~110 Hz, Hal Saflieni Hypogeum ~110-114 Hz, King's Chamber)

### Takeaway
Measurements of 95–120 Hz resonances in British Isles chambers and ~110–114 Hz in the Hypogeum are real and peer-reviewed or at least published. The contested step is intent: Wright (2007) argues such resonances are expected from chamber size and Weyl's formula and are no stronger evidence of design than a bathroom. Brain-effect work at ~110 Hz (Cook et al. 2008) is a 30-person pilot with no replication I found. King's Chamber claims are mostly advocate/informal, and the one recent peer-reviewed pyramid vibration study measured ~2 Hz structural vibrations, not audio resonance.

### Cited Findings
**Jahn, Devereux & Ibison (1996)**
- Citation: Jahn, Devereux & Ibison, "Acoustical resonances of assorted ancient structures", *J. Acoust. Soc. Am.* 99(2): 649-658 (1996). Companion: Devereux & Jahn, "Preliminary investigations and cognitive considerations of the acoustical resonances of selected archaeological sites", *Antiquity* 70(269): 665-666, DOI 10.1017/S0003598X00083800. [PRIMARY; citation details via Cambridge abstract page] — [Antiquity (Cambridge)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/abs/preliminary-investigations-and-cognitive-considerations-of-the-acoustical-resonances-of-selected-archaeological-sites/D64ECE9DCBFEAE665001E2033D61A7D1). The Antiquity abstract names the Hypogeum as the most resonant site for chanting a rhythmic "Oum" and asks whether acoustic features of northern European chambers were integral to design.
- Six British Isles chambers were measured (five ~3500 BC, one ~500 BC), including Wayland's Smithy, Chun Quoit, Cairn Euny, Newgrange, Cairns L and I, and Carrowkeel/"Carbane West" per one source; each showed a resonance between 95 and 120 Hz despite differing shapes (cruciform, rectangular, beehive, petal). Authors speculated ritual chanting. [NEUTRAL summary of the study] — [ASA / Wright 2007](https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/153rd/wright.html); site list and 1994-95 dating from [Viewzone article, ADVOCATE-leaning](https://dcmpx.remotevs.com/com/viewzone/PL/archeosound.html). Names of sites differ slightly between sources; I did not read the JASA paper itself.
- A Devereux slide deck lists Newgrange at 110 Hz as part of the 1995-96 ICRL project. [PRIMARY/ADVOCATE slides] — [Gresham College slides](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/sites/default/files/watch-downloads/2019-01-14_TomService_SacredListening_PP-PaulDevereux.pdf)
- Jahn and Ibison were associated with the Princeton Engineering Anomalies Research (PEAR) lab; per Wright this was PEAR's only archaeoacoustics work. [CRITICAL context] — [Wright 2007](https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/153rd/wright.html)

**Critical response (Wright, 2007)**
- Matthew Wright (ISVR, Southampton), "Is a Neolithic Burial Chamber Different from My Bathroom, Acoustically Speaking?", presented at the 153rd ASA meeting, 6 June 2007 (paper 3pAA4). Using Weyl's formula (1912), resonances depend on volume, wall area and edge length, not exact shape; for conservative chamber sizes there is more than one resonance in all but the smallest chamber (just under seven in the largest). Resonances in 95-120 Hz were expected regardless of design. After adjusting for reflective floors and openings, evidence for deliberate chanting design is "no stronger than for a typical bathroom". He also notes no other evidence of chanting. [CRITICAL, conference lay-language paper; not a full peer-reviewed article as far as I found] — [Wright 2007](https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/153rd/wright.html)
- Wright does not evaluate the authors' suggested similarity between rock art and resonance mode shapes. [same source]

**Hal Saflieni Hypogeum (Malta)**
- Debertolis, Coimbra & Eneix (2015), *Journal of Anthropology and Archaeology* 3(1): 59-79: field experiment 21 Feb 2014 in the Oracle Room; double resonance ~114 Hz and 68-70 Hz (authors expected ~110-111 Hz from earlier studies). Male voice and strongly struck Irish drum stimulated it; conch, shouted voices, bull horn did not. Caveats: one room, one session, small number of performers, no statistical analysis, subjective claims acknowledged, EEG claims cited but not presented, builders' intentions "can never be fully known". [PRIMARY, with its own stated limitations; summary via fetch tool] — [Debertolis et al. 2015 PDF](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/16630/1/OA%20Archaeoacoustic%20Analysis%20of%20the%20%C4%A6al%20Saflieni%20Hypogeum%20in%20Malta.pdf)
- Maltese temple rooms tested by the Old Temples Study Foundation resonated at 110 or 111 Hz (advocate-linked source; "Oracle Chamber's 110 Hz resonance is 'suspected'" per the sensory-studies page). [ADVOCATE] — [Viewzone](https://dcmpx.remotevs.com/com/viewzone/PL/archeosound.html); [SensoryStudies.org](https://www.sensorystudies.org/?p=336)

**Cook, Pajot & Leuchter (2008)**
- "Ancient Architectural Acoustic Resonance Patterns and Regional Brain Activity", *Time and Mind* 1(1): 95-104 (March 2008). Pilot study of 30 healthy adults hearing tones at 90, 100, 110, 120, 130 Hz with EEG. At 110 Hz, left temporal activity was markedly lower than at other frequencies, and prefrontal activity shifted from left- to right-dominant. Authors call results preliminary and call for a larger study. [PRIMARY via secondary summary] — [Earth Notes bibliography](https://www.earth.org.uk/bibliography/cook2008resonance.html); same description in [Viewzone](https://dcmpx.remotevs.com/com/viewzone/PL/archeosound.html). Note: this tested tones in a lab, not people in a chamber; I found no replication.

**Other archaeoacoustics evidence and critiques**
- Chavín de Huántar (Kolar et al., Stanford): a resonance within the site matched that produced by pututu shell trumpets also used in Chavín culture. [NEUTRAL] — [Wikipedia: Archaeoacoustics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeoacoustics)
- Steven Waller (2011) claimed Stonehenge's layout mimics a sound interference pattern from two sources; Science News (2012) called it "tantalizing, but fantastical", citing "several leaps of unsubstantiated logic", missing controls and little science behind it. [CRITICAL] — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/blog/scene/archaeoacoustics-tantalizing-fantastical)
- Watson & Keating (1999), *Antiquity*: "Architecture and sound: an acoustic analysis of megalithic monuments in prehistoric Britain" (title only confirmed). Watson's project began 1995 at Easter Aquorthies; he reports chambered monuments (e.g., Maeshowe, Newgrange) give resonances and open sites (Stonehenge, Ring of Brodgar) give echoes. [PRIMARY, summary page] — [Aaron Watson](https://aaronwatson.co.uk/acoustics-intro)
- Field-level assessment: Science News says the field is compelling but not yet rigorous. Wikipedia notes method differences between artefact and site studies and a dependence on funding for continuity. [CRITICAL / NEUTRAL] — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/blog/scene/archaeoacoustics-tantalizing-fantastical); [Wikipedia: Archaeoacoustics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeoacoustics)

**King's Chamber, Great Pyramid**
- Tom Danley (acoustic engineer) described measuring the King's Chamber (~12.2 x 6.1 x 6.1 m, with ≥61 m of stone around it); in the excerpt available he gave only rough estimates (granite beams ~300 Hz; Helmholtz/transmission-line modelling suggesting resonances starting ~2.5 Hz) and described the room as very "live". No measured resonance numbers in the available portion; no independent verification. [PRIMARY informal trade-magazine piece] — [ProSoundWeb](https://www.prosoundweb.com/the-great-pyramid-early-reflections-ancient-echoes/4/)
- A recent structural (not audio) study: ScienceAlert reports a *Scientific Reports* 2026 paper (DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-49962-6; lead Asem Salama, NRIAG Egypt) using 37 accelerometers on ambient vibrations: ground ~0.6 Hz, most of the pyramid ~2.0-2.6 Hz, amplification rising with height, and sharply lower amplification in the relieving chambers above the King's Chamber. ScienceAlert says the intentional-design reading is speculative; it reports no King's Chamber acoustic resonance finding. [NEUTRAL, secondary] — [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/the-great-pyramid-has-survived-4600-years-a-strange-feature-may-help-explain-why). Futura-Sciences gives the same figures but names the journal as *Nature*, a conflict with ScienceAlert; treat the journal as unresolved and check the DOI. [NEUTRAL, secondary, conflicting] — [Futura-Sciences](https://www.futura-sciences.com/en/?p=34031)
- Advocate pages claim the pyramid was a precision resonator ("forbidden frequency"); I did not fetch or verify them and the searches returned mainly social/Substack posts, so I treat these as unvetted advocate claims.

### Inferences
- Defensible statement: "Several Neolithic chambers measured in 1990s-2010s studies show a strong low-frequency resonance near 95-120 Hz, within the male voice range." Not defensible without qualification: "built to resonate at 110 Hz."
- Wright's Weyl-formula argument is a general-statistics critique, not a refutation: it shows resonances were likely regardless of intent, but does not prove intent was absent. Jahn/Devereux's inference of intent is untested. Both sides' strongest position is "unresolved".
- Cook et al. gives, at most, a hypothesis-generating pilot linking ~110 Hz tones to EEG changes. It should not be used to claim health or mood benefits of resonant spaces.

### Gaps
- I could not open the JASA 1996 paper or full Time and Mind text; details (exact per-site frequencies, sample, controls) come from secondary summaries.
- No peer-reviewed measurement of the King's Chamber audible resonance was found, and Reid's pyramid analysis claims were not located.
- Newgrange-specific independent measurements beyond Jahn's 110 Hz were not retrieved (one fetched page about Dowth South/Fourknocks I did not give the values).
- No full text of Wright's critique in a peer-reviewed journal was found; only the ASA lay-language version.

## 4. Real architecture and urban projects derived from cymatic or standing-wave patterns; generative design with Chladni figures; acoustic design of buildings

### Takeaway
Genuinely built "cymatic-derived" architecture appears to be small-scale and exploratory (installations, temporary structures, student/research-through-design projects). I found no major permanent building or urban plan whose form is documented as derived from cymatic patterns with performance evidence. By contrast, standing-wave and modal analysis is mainstream in actual acoustic design (rooms, concert halls, instruments).

### Cited Findings
- A cymatics-driven temporary shelter: water driven by sine waves at ~5-200 Hz was photographed to produce forms for an icosahedral bamboo reciprocal-structure shelter (Chilean coligüe), a 1:5 model, and a stated use as a concert backstage space in Concepción in 2020. Paper reports no quantitative mapping from pattern to geometry, no structural or performance data, contains internal inconsistencies (e.g., 38 Hz vs 100 Hz in one figure), and calls its results preliminary. [PRIMARY, research-through-design; limitations as read from the paper] — [IAFOR ECADE 2022](https://papers.iafor.org/wp-content/uploads/papers/ecade2022/ECADE2022_63362.pdf)
- Search results also surfaced other cymatics-in-design items (a Chalmers thesis on translating soundscape into responsive installation, surface-design experiments, a Springer chapter, patent for "cymatic projection"). I did not open them, so I make no claims about them. — [search result list]
- Chladni patterns in instrument making are real engineering practice: makers use plate mode patterns as feedback while thinning plates and braces on guitar and violin tops ("Symmetrical plates give symmetrical patterns"); Hutchins and Voskuil's *Mode Tuning for the Violin Maker* is cited. [NEUTRAL, UNSW physics] — [UNSW guitar Chladni page](https://newt.phys.unsw.edu.au/music/guitar/guitarchladni.html). This is a verified use of the principle, but for instruments, not buildings.
- Wittkower/Ouvrard musical-proportion theory is documented design history (see Q2).
- Acoustic design of real buildings uses geometrical and wave acoustics rather than cymatics: e.g., Epidaurus theatre, where Declercq & Dekeyser (Georgia Tech, JASA 2007) found the stepped limestone seat rows act as an acoustic filter damping frequencies below ~500 Hz (suppressing low-frequency noise while speech stays audible); it is unclear whether this was intentional, and a seated audience would change the effect. [NEUTRAL, Nature news, paper then "in press"] — [Nature News](https://www.nature.com/news/2007/070319/full/news070319-16.html)

### Inferences
- A "cymatic" generator can legitimately be described as a *form-generation or aesthetic method* (Chladni/Faraday-inspired geometry), which has small built precedents. It has no documented track record of delivering acoustic, structural or wayfinding performance gains in buildings or cities.
- The more rigorous analogues are modal analysis (finite-element eigenmodes), which are standard engineering, but that is a different claim from cymatics-as-sacred-geometry.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed example of a permanent building or city plan derived from cymatic figures with measured outcomes was found; absence in my limited search is not proof none exists.
- Well-known parametric/"Chladni" facade or pavilion projects by named architecture firms were not verified (search results were dominated by academic or patent items I did not open).

## 5. Real-world benefits of radial/symmetric layouts that resemble cymatic patterns (acoustic, structural, wayfinding)

### Takeaway
Radial/circular geometry has real, documented acoustic consequences, but many are *problems* (focusing, caustics, flutter echo) unless deliberately managed. Wayfinding evidence in street networks is mixed: regularity helps some measures, but one GPS study found heuristic popularity barely differed between a regular and an irregular network. I found no evidence that cymatic-pattern resemblance per se confers benefits.

### Cited Findings
**Acoustics of circular/curved geometry**
- Curved reflectors (elliptical, parabolic, circular, domed) focus sound and create uneven high/low-intensity zones; design advice is to avoid focusing shapes in auditoria, add diffusing elements, and avoid large flat reflectors and parallel walls (flutter echo). [NEUTRAL, HyperPhysics] — [HyperPhysics](https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/Acoustic/reflc.html)
- Kulowski (2018, *Applied Acoustics* 133: 82-90): in circular rooms, directional sources near the wall create concentric caustics that explain whispering-gallery effects such as St Paul's; vaults act as acoustic mirrors; in auditoria caustics are usually a flaw, but they can be used deliberately (Whispering Grottoes at Oliwa; Mormon Tabernacle balcony placed at caustic height). Analysis uses geometrical acoustics. [PRIMARY peer-reviewed, via summary] — [Kulowski 2018](https://pub.pg.edu.pl/en/download/143731-the-caustic-in-the-acoustics-of-historic-interiors)
- Epidaurus: a stepped, radial seating bowl with documented low-frequency filtering benefit (see Q4). Here the benefit comes from the stepped-row geometry and stone, not from a cymatic pattern. [NEUTRAL] — [Nature News](https://www.nature.com/news/2007/070319/full/news070319-16.html)

**Wayfinding and street networks**
- Bhowmick, Winter, Stevenson & Vortisch (2020), *J. Spatial Information Science* 21: 203-228: GPS-recorded walking trips in Beijing (irregular) and Melbourne (regular). Overall heuristic popularity barely differed; where routes differed, shortest-leg-first and least-angle strategies were preferred; Melbourne trips had lower mean route length and Hausdorff distance. Only two cities; sample size not stated on the page. [PRIMARY, peer-reviewed] — [JOSIS](https://josis.org/index.php/josis/article/view/139)
- Geoff Boeing's blog (commentary using Lynch's legibility ideas) notes Boston's non-uniform street orientation makes it harder for visitors to navigate, but local landmarks, edges and districts make it "imageable". Boeing attributes its layout to age, terrain and annexation. [NEUTRAL, expert blog] — [Boeing 2018](https://geoffboeing.com/2018/07/comparing-city-street-orientations/)

### Inferences
- Plausible real benefits of a radial or symmetric plan: central legibility (a clear hub), predictable orientation, and in theatre-type geometry, sightline and sound-delivery advantages. These come from geometry (distance to center, line of sight, stepped surfaces), not from resemblance to cymatic figures.
- Real costs: acoustic focusing in concave circular spaces, long radial distances to non-hub destinations, and the fact that 2D symmetric patterns in plates are driven by plate boundary conditions, not by anything the city has analogously.
- The wayfinding evidence base I found is thin and does not specifically test radial or cymatic layouts.

### Gaps
- No studies directly comparing radial/concentric layouts to grids on wayfinding were retrieved (searches returned space-syntax and network-regularity papers I did not open: e.g., Hölscher-group work, Lynch-based legibility).
- No evidence found linking symmetry resembling cymatic nodal patterns to structural advantages (e.g., load paths in domes are a separate engineering domain I did not research).
- No sources on psychological/health benefits of such layouts were examined.

## Suggested honest-basis language for a design engine (derived from the above; my synthesis, not sourced claims)
- Supported: "Patterns are generated from standing-wave (Chladni/Faraday-style) mathematics; the geometry is real, reproducible physics."
