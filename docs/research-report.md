# Turning Frequency Into Plausible Cymatic Cities

An engine can reliably turn a chosen frequency into a city layout, but only by treating the frequency as a seed for real standing-wave mathematics and the city as a design layer on top of it. The physics is solid and deterministic. A frequency selects a wavenumber through a dispersion law (**f ∝ k for a membrane, f ∝ k² for a plate, f ∝ √(l(l+1)) for a sphere**), the wavenumber selects one or a few eigenmodes, and the zero set and extrema of that mode give the geometry: rings, spokes, diagonals, grids. Plausibility does not come from the wave, because nothing in a nodal pattern knows about street widths or gravity. It comes from a second rule layer: street, block and density budgets taken from real planning standards, tensor-field street tracing that follows the pattern's contours, and structural limits (slenderness and gravity-scaled height) that decide what can stand. The evidence for cymatic or "harmonic" architecture itself is thin. The patterns are real physics, but I found no built city or permanent building derived from cymatic figures with measured performance. Claims that 432 Hz, Solfeggio tones or the 7.83 Hz Schumann resonance make places "harmonic" have no documented basis, and the best-supported archaeoacoustic finding (roughly 95-120 Hz chamber resonances) has an unresolved intent question. The engine should therefore label cymatic generation as a form-generation method and keep any frequency symbolism in the fiction.

## The frequency fixes a wavenumber, and the domain's dispersion law fixes the pattern

Every cymatic figure is an eigenmode of a wave equation on a bounded domain, and the domain determines how frequency maps to pattern complexity. For an ideal circular membrane the modes are u = J_m(j_{m,n} r/R)·cos(mθ), with frequency f = j_{m,n}·c/(2πR), so frequency is linear in wavenumber. The index m counts nodal diameters and n counts nodal circles, and the circle radii are the ratios of Bessel zeros, r_i = R·j_{m,i}/j_{m,n} (a derivation in the research notes; the zeros themselves interlace per [DLMF §10.21](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.21)). The first zero j₀,₁ is 2.4048, so a membrane with R = 0.1 m and wave speed c = 100 m/s has its fundamental at 382.74 Hz (my computation, scipy). The modes are inharmonic, with frequency ratios 1, 1.593, 2.136, 2.295 and so on, which is why a drum does not sound like a string. A real Chladni plate is different. It obeys the thin-plate equation ρh·w_tt + D∇⁴w = 0, which gives ω = k²·√(D/ρh) ([Dalessi et al. 2021](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~sdalessi/SN-AppliedSciences2021.pdf)). Chladni's empirical law f ∝ (m+2n)^p with p ≈ 2 is that dispersion in disguise, and Kirchhoff showed it holds asymptotically at high frequency (same source). At low order it is only qualitative. Fitting the computed free-circular-plate eigenvalues gives an exponent near 2.4, so an engine needing real ratios should tabulate eigenvalues rather than use the law. The free-plate values λ² = 5.358, 9.003, 12.439, 20.475 and 21.835 for the first five modes (ν = 0.3) are my own root-finding results, not a quoted table. The practical consequence of dispersion is that membrane pattern complexity grows in proportion to frequency, while plate pattern complexity grows only like √f, so a plate-based generator produces gently growing complexity across a wide frequency range.

On a sphere the eigenfunctions are spherical harmonics Y_l^m with Laplace-Beltrami eigenvalue −l(l+1), giving 2l+1 degenerate modes per degree ([Wikipedia: Spherical harmonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_harmonics)). A scalar-wave model then gives f_l = (c/2πR)·√(l(l+1)), with nodal lines made of exactly l circles, |m| of them meridians and l−|m| of them latitude circles (same source). That is the natural model for spherical or planetary megastructures. It is a model, not a thin-shell result. Real shells have two frequency branches per degree ([Abaqus Benchmarks](https://ceae-server.colorado.edu/v2016/books/bmk/ch01s04ach37.html)), and a droplet follows Rayleigh's ω² ∝ l(l−1)(l+2) ([arXiv 1905.05844](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1905.05844)). The research found no retrievable closed form for a true thin spherical shell, so the engine should label the sphere as idealised.

| Domain | Dispersion | Mode shape | Complexity growth | Degeneracy handling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Disk membrane | f ∝ j_{m,n} (linear in k) | Bessel J_m × cos(mθ) | ∝ f | rotation free for m ≥ 1 |
| Free plate | f ∝ k² (quadratic) | Bessel J + modified Bessel I (circle); Ritz or cos–cos± (square) | ∝ √f | symmetric pair, mix set by drive point |
| Sphere (scalar) | f ∝ √(l(l+1)) | Y_l^m | l nodal circles | 2l+1 fold, m set by drive position |

Degeneracy is the engine's main reproducibility trap, and the notes give a clean rule. When two modes share a frequency, the excited pattern is the sum weighted by each mode's value at the drive point. For a square plate with a centre driver, cos(nπx/L)cos(mπy/L) + cos(mπx/L)cos(nπy/L) is excited and the minus combination is not, because it vanishes at the centre ([Georgi, LibreTexts](https://phys.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Waves_and_Acoustics/The_Physics_of_Waves_(Goergi)/11%3A_Two_and_Three_Dimensions/11.03%3A_Chladni_Plates)). Drive position (or a seed) is therefore a legitimate second input beside frequency, and fixing it makes the output deterministic. This cos–cos family reproduces the classic visual vocabulary of diagonals, grids and curved diamonds, but it only satisfies zero-slope edges, not the true free-edge conditions. Real nodal lines curve near boundaries, so it is a generator for plausible-looking figures rather than accurate frequencies ([Wheatstone excerpt](https://web.mit.edu/redingtn/www/netadv/SP20140804.html)).

Real drive frequencies rarely sit exactly on a mode, and the model for that is a damped modal sum. Each mode's weight is |⟨φ_n,F⟩| / √((ω_n²−ω²)² + γ_n²ω²), with Q = ω_n/γ_n, so the pattern morphs smoothly between neighbouring modes as frequency sweeps ([arXiv 2008.02040](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.02040)). A worked case in the notes: two modes at 1,000 and 1,100 Hz with Q = 50, driven at 1,050 Hz, contribute almost equally at about a fifth of resonant amplitude (my computation). A modest Q of 30-100 gives readable morphing, and a high Q gives pure modes only in narrow windows. Real plate-plus-driver systems also differ from free-plate theory. Tuan et al. found that the driver acts as a point scatterer, "modern" figures form at resonances with avoided crossings of nodal lines, and "classical" grid-like figures form off resonance ([EPL 2015](https://epljournal.edpsciences.org/10.1209/0295-5075/111/64004)). Finally, sand collects on nodal lines because grains hop off antinodes once plate acceleration exceeds g, so the density map is bright where |w| is small. That rule inverts for very fine powder or gentle drive below g ([van Gerner et al. 2010](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevE.82.012301); [Aranson & Tsimring](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0507419)). For city generation this gives the engine a documented choice: build on nodal sets (settlement collects where the field is quiet) or on antinodes (the inverse pattern).

## From a scalar field to streets, blocks and 3D form

The notes establish the physics and the urban-generation literature but not a published cymatics-to-city pipeline, so this section is a design proposal assembled from those cited parts. The best-fitting street method is Chen et al.'s tensor-field modelling, which builds the field's minor eigenvector from a height gradient, using θ = arctan(∂H/∂y ÷ ∂H/∂x) + π/2 and magnitude |∇H|, so major hyperstreamlines run along contours ([Chen et al. 2008](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~zhange/images/street_sig08.pdf)). Feed it the standing-wave amplitude as H and the result is ring roads along the contours and connector roads up the slope. Blending with grid and radial basis fields, using Gaussian-decay weights, keeps flat regions from losing their street pattern. The topology follows from the field. Singular points sit at peaks, pits and saddles, and around a central extremum the major streamlines become concentric rings and the minor ones spokes, the same structure as Palmanova or Karlsruhe (derived in the notes). A field with several extrema yields several radial nuclei, comparable to Canberra's multi-centre plan. Parish and Müller's L-system approach is better reserved for road hierarchy, lot subdivision and per-lot building generation, since it responds to maps through local growth rules rather than a global field ([Purdue course notes](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/urban/sg_2011_course/umc_SG11_02_urban_layouts.pdf)). The notes could not find numeric spacing, look-ahead or decay parameters for Chen's method, so those are tuning constants, not sourced values.

Mapping field quantities to urban roles then follows the physics. One workable assignment is nodal lines as ring boulevards, walls or canals, antinode lobes as districts or plazas, and amplitude as a density driver that scales building height. This is an aesthetic mapping with no physical justification beyond the sand analogy. For three dimensions the researched routes are extrusion of the plate field into a height map (a gentle option), spherical harmonics for globe or orbital habitats (nodal circles become latitude bands and meridian ribs), and radial scaling by the mode's index. The notes did not cover full three-dimensional cavity modes. What exists on that topic is Weyl's formula, as used by Wright, which says room resonances depend on volume, wall area and edge length rather than exact shape ([Wright 2007](https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/153rd/wright.html)). That is a caution against reading a building's shape as encoding a frequency. A 3D lattice of nodal surfaces would be an unsupported extension of the method, though a reasonable creative one.

## Planning, structure and gravity set what counts as plausible

A generator that only draws nodal lines will produce beautiful, uninhabitable layouts. The notes yield enough real constraints to check its output. UN-Habitat recommends at least 30% of city land for streets, at least 18 km of street per km², at least 150 people per hectare, and a land-use mix of roughly 40-60% economic, 30-50% residential and about 10% public services ([UN-Habitat](https://unhabitat.org/sites/default/files/download-manager-files/A%20New%20Strategy%20of%20Sustainable%20Neighbourhood%20Planning%20Five%20principles.pdf)). Its public-space guidance of 30-35% streets and 15-20% open space comes from [Next City](https://nextcity.org/urbanist-news/how-much-public-space-does-a-city-need-UN-Habitat-joan-clos-50-percent). Real block sizes range from about 80 × 274 m in Manhattan to 100 × 200 m in Chicago and Melbourne ([Wikipedia: City block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_block)), and ring-radial plans must either add spokes at larger radii or accept wedge-shaped parcels, because parcel width grows with radius (inference in the notes). Historical radial cities supply parameter sets: Palmanova has nine bastions and three nine-sided rings ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmanova)), Karlsruhe has 32 radial streets fanning from a palace tower ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe)), and Nahalal has eight equal sectors and 80 parcels ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahalal)). They are also mostly partial: Karlsruhe is a fan rather than a full circle, and Canberra's rings are hexagonal and octagonal and bent to the terrain ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra)). A generator should truncate perfect symmetry with water, terrain and a few diagonal overlays in the way L'Enfant's plan did ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Enfant_Plan)).

For height, the notes give a usable slenderness ladder. Engineers call a tower slender above about 1:10 to 1:12, 432 Park Avenue reaches about 1:15, and the thinnest tower in the notes, 111 West 57th Street (435 m), reaches about 1:24 ([Architectural Record](https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/15106-a-cliff-dwellers-guide-to-tall-skinny-buildings)). The free-tier and hard-cap thresholds around those anchors are my own choices. Burj Khalifa shows how supertalls manage wind, using a buttressed core, a Y plan and 27 spiral setbacks to disrupt vortex shedding ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burj_Khalifa)). For alien worlds the cleanest rule is the self-weight compression limit h = σ/(ρg), which scales as 1/g, giving roughly 3.4 km for granite on Earth, 9 km on Mars and 21 km on the Moon from the cited formula ([Jay Maron](https://jaymaron.com/oom/mountains.html)). A safety factor and effective-density correction are mine and were not sourced. The notes also flag that this prismatic-column formula overstates what real buildings do, and that wind, not crushing, usually governs on Earth. Megastructure proposals show what taper and aspect ratio look like at scale. X-Seed 4000 is 4,000 m tall on a 6 km platform, wider than it is tall ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-Seed_4000)), and Sky City 1000 is 1,000 m on a 400 m base ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_City_1000)). Both are unbuilt. Their average gross height of about 5 m per floor, against 3-4 m typical, implies large mechanical and sky-lobby allowances (derived).

Megastructure realism is also a social and logistical question. The Line (170 km long, 200 m wide, 500 m tall, nine million planned residents) has been scaled back, and a Vienna Complexity Science Hub study found average distances between residents of about 57 km in a linear city against 2.9 km in a circular city of equal area ([Wikipedia: The Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Line_(Neom))). A radial cymatic layout is therefore inherently better on walkability than a stripe pattern. Real outcomes also fall far below the plan: Masdar was designed for 45,000-50,000 residents and had about 5,000 residents by 2023 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masdar_City)), and Arcosanti planned for up to 5,000 but typically holds 50-150 people ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcosanti)). A plausibility layer can therefore apply a 15-minute-walk constraint to daily life, vertical transport as a prerequisite for high density, and occupancy well below capacity for ruined or failing alien cities.

## What is and is not supported about cymatic and harmonic architecture

The phenomenon is real and fully explained by classical physics. Chladni published systematic plate figures in 1787, Hooke saw the effect in 1680, and Faraday described waves in vibrated liquids in 1831 ([APS News](https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2017/07/first-experiments-chladni-figures); [Wikipedia: Cymatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cymatics)). Hans Jenny coined "cymatics" and documented forms in powders and liquids. His reading of them as a "force field of vibrational energy" is philosophical, and nothing found shows he made architectural claims ([Wikipedia: Cymatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cymatics)). The CymaScope's own pages list 16 application fields but give no studies or data ([CymaScope](https://www.cymascope.com)). On the built side, the notes found only small exploratory work, such as a bamboo reciprocal-structure shelter with a self-described preliminary study and no structural or performance data ([IAFOR ECADE 2022](https://papers.iafor.org/wp-content/uploads/papers/ecade2022/ECADE2022_63362.pdf)). The notes found no permanent building or city plan derived from cymatic figures with measured outcomes, though they caution that limited search cannot prove none exists. Chladni patterns do have a verified engineering use in instrument making, where makers use plate-mode patterns to tune guitar and violin plates ([UNSW](https://newt.phys.unsw.edu.au/music/guitar/guitarchladni.html)). That is a different scale and purpose from a city.

The frequency-numerology claims fare worse. No 432 Hz standard existed before 440 Hz, and European pitch had ranged from about 409 to 457 Hz over time ([Lead Stories](https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2022/10/fact-check-tuning-standard-of-a-440-hz-was-not-driven-by-nazi-and-rockefeller-desire-for-discord.html)). The "scientific pitch" with C4 = 256 Hz gives A4 ≈ 430.54 Hz, not 432 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_pitch)). The only direct 432 versus 440 study reported was a small non-randomised trial ([ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/432hz-brain-tuning-is-an-ancient-idea-does-it-actually-work)). The Solfeggio set was reportedly derived in the 1970s from verse numbers in the Book of Numbers and has no clinical support ([Science Feedback](https://science.feedback.org/review/no-evidence-support-claim-solfeggio-frequencies-remove-toxins-from-body/)). Schumann resonance is a real electromagnetic phenomenon, but a 7.83 Hz acoustic wave would be about 43 km long in air (my arithmetic), so it cannot be a room or city resonance, and its human-health link is unestablished ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schumann_resonances_conspiracy_theories)). The strongest empirical case is archaeoacoustic. Jahn, Devereux and Ibison reported resonances of 95-120 Hz in six British Isles chambers ([JASA 1996, via ASA summary](https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/153rd/wright.html)), and a Hypogeum field test found double resonances near 114 and 68-70 Hz with stated limits of one room, one session and no statistics ([Debertolis et al. 2015](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/16630/1/OA%20Archaeoacoustic%20Analysis%20of%20the%20%C4%A6al%20Saflieni%20Hypogeum%20in%20Malta.pdf)). Wright's counterpoint is that Weyl's formula predicts such resonances for chambers of those sizes regardless of design, so evidence for intent is "no stronger than for a typical bathroom" ([Wright 2007](https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/153rd/wright.html)). A 30-person EEG pilot at 110 Hz was preliminary and unreplicated as far as the notes could find ([Earth Notes](https://www.earth.org.uk/bibliography/cook2008resonance.html)). Intent remains unresolved, and no health claim follows.

The one documented link between music and architectural form is ratio theory. Ouvrard's *Architecture Harmonique* (1679) maps musical consonances onto proportions, and a 2023 analysis finds the correlations internally accurate but the claimed infallibility unsupported ([Taraldsen, Nexus Network Journal](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00004-022-00625-9)). That is a legitimate historical design tradition, not evidence that buildings resonate at pitches. The claimed benefits of radial layouts are geometric, not cymatic. Circular rooms focus sound into caustics and whispering-gallery effects, usually a flaw unless used deliberately ([Kulowski 2018](https://pub.pg.edu.pl/en/download/143731-the-caustic-in-the-acoustics-of-historic-interiors)), and Epidaurus's stepped limestone seating filters frequencies below about 500 Hz ([Nature News](https://www.nature.com/news/2007/070319/full/news070319-16.html)). One GPS wayfinding study found that regular and irregular street networks barely differed in heuristic popularity ([Bhowmick et al. 2020](https://josis.org/index.php/josis/article/view/139)). The evidence base on wayfinding is thin, and none of it tests cymatic layouts.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. "Cymatic city" works as a generative grammar in which frequency is a legitimate, reproducible input that fixes a wavenumber, a mode and a nodal topology, and drive position, Q and boundary shape add controllable variety. It fails as a claim of performance or harmony. The most defensible engine therefore separates three layers: a physics layer (modes, dispersion, degeneracy, driven response), a planning layer (street and density budgets, tensor-field routing, ring-radial parameters, truncation by terrain) and a structural layer (slenderness caps and 1/g-scaled height). Only the first is cymatic. The other two make the output look like a place people could live in, and the engine should say plainly which layer is responsible for which result.

For alien and megastructure cities, the interesting lever is the planetary parameter rather than the frequency: changing g, atmosphere and wave speed rescales the structure limits and the pattern scale while the mode topology stays fixed. Gaps remain that a build will have to fill by tuning. The notes lack sourced street-width classes, residential area per person, maximum road grade, wind-sway limits, thin-shell sphere formulas and any worked three-dimensional cavity-mode generator. Several numbers (free-plate eigenvalues, Ritz square-plate values, gravity-scaled heights) are my own computations or derivations that the engine's authors should verify against Leissa's tables and a structural reference before treating them as authoritative.
